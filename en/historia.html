Our History

A tradition of more than 800 years, rooted in Acre during the Crusades and revived as a contemporary Christian Masonic organisation.

Foundations of the Order

Who was St Thomas

Thomas Becket became Archbishop of Canterbury in 1162. His conflict with King Henry II concerned the rights and independence of the Church. On 29 December 1170 he was killed in Canterbury Cathedral by four knights. Canonised in 1173, Becket became an important religious figure in medieval England, and his tomb drew pilgrims. It is this St Thomas, also known as Thomas of Canterbury, whose memory is associated with the Order.

Note: Becket lived before the events at Acre in 1191. The Order commemorates him; he did not found the contemporary Masonic organisation. He should not be confused with the Apostle Thomas or with St Thomas Aquinas.

Acre and the Tradition of Service

According to the tradition preserved by the Order, at Acre during the Third Crusade in 1191, William, a cleric associated with the Dean of St Paul’s Cathedral in London, gathered helpers to care for the wounded, bury the dead and raise funds to ransom captives. The dedication of a church and burial ground to Thomas Becket connected this initiative with the memory of the English saint. This tradition of service also developed a chivalric character.

Acre is the historic city on the eastern Mediterranean, also known as Akko. “Acon” is the historic English form of Acre, and is the origin of the name the Order uses.

The Contemporary Order

The contemporary Masonic organisation grew out of the work of John E. N. Walker, who spent approximately twenty years researching records in the Guildhall Library in London. An account of a Master’s installation in 1510 inspired the commemorative revival of the Order. It was constituted under a charter from the Grand Master’s Council in 1973 and established in its contemporary form in 1974, distinct from its medieval history.

Motto: Humilitas et Caritas (Humility and Charity). The motto expresses service, compassion and assistance to others. The Order is Christian and Masonic, is organised into Chapels, and admits eligible Masonic Knights Templar by invitation.

Origins of the Province of South America

In January 2019 a delegation from the Central Authority travelled to Rio de Janeiro to consecrate Chapels No. 129, 130 and 132 and to constitute the Province of South America. Sir Alexander of Rio de Janeiro was the first Grand Preceptor. The Grand Master at the time was Sir Melvyn of Wud Plumpton.

The first Brazilian Chapel was consecrated on 16 June 2014 in São Paulo. With the formal constitution of the Province in January 2019, the Order began a new chapter, bringing the English and Christian chivalric tradition to South America under an established provincial leadership.

“A tradition of Christian chivalry, memory and service, preserved today through its Chapels and carried forward in South America by its own Provincial structure.”
