PROVINCE OF SOUTH AMERICA

History

Remembering the past.
A commitment to service.

The account of 1191 is presented as a tradition preserved by the Order. It is distinct from the constitution of the contemporary Masonic organisation in 1973 and 1974.

1170–1173

The remembrance of Thomas Becket

The martyrdom and canonisation of Thomas Becket provide the background to the later dedication of the church at Acre and the English remembrance of the saint.

1191

The tradition of Acre

According to the tradition preserved by the Order, William, a cleric associated with the Dean of St Paul's, gathered helpers to care for the wounded, bury the dead and free captives. This founding memory centres on service in the face of suffering.

13th–14th centuries

Hospitality and chivalry

The history encompasses a hospital, church, ransom of captives and a later military character. Records from this period are fragmentary.

Cheapside · London

An English headquarters

The English headquarters occupied property associated with the Becket family. The Mercers form part of the site's material history.

1367 / 1510

The surviving records

The brief identifies the last known admission in 1367 and a record of a Master's installation in 1510, which would become central to the contemporary revival.

1538 / 1666

Dissolution and remembrance

Dissolution, acquisition by the Mercers and the Great Fire of London mark distinct stages in the building's history and remembrance.

1973–1974

The contemporary Order

Following some twenty years of research by John E. N. Walker at the Guildhall Library, the Order was constituted in 1973 and organised in its contemporary form in 1974.

2014

The first documented Brazilian Chapel

On 16 June, Grão-Mestre Desembargador Murilo Pinto Chapel No. 113 was consecrated in São Paulo.

2019

The Province of South America

The central team's visit from 11 to 15 January marked the consecration of three Chapels and the constitution of the Province in Rio de Janeiro. Sir Alexander of Rio de Janeiro was invested as the first Grand Preceptor.
